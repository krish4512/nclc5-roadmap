NCLC 5 Roadmap

Everything to cover to hit NCLC / CLB 5 (CEFR B1) on either TCF Canada or TEF Canada — the score bands you're aiming for, the exam format, and a checklist of grammar, vocabulary and skills by section. Check items off as you go; your progress is saved on this page.

Grammar learning order

The same 21 structures below, but sequenced — each stage leans on the one above it. Work top to bottom; inside a stage, order doesn't matter. Tap any item to open its conjugation table, a tip for learning it fast, and an example sentence.

parle — underlined letters in the tables are silent. Say the word without them.

How to learn this the easiest way

  1. Active recall beats rereading. Cover the table, try to reproduce it from memory, then check — don't just stare at it until it looks familiar.
  2. Produce before you move on. Write 3 original sentences with a new structure immediately after learning it. Recognizing it in a table and using it under pressure are different skills.
  3. Learn verb tables by pattern, not verb-by-verb. The 6 endings for -er verbs are one thing to learn, not 30 things — drill the pattern, then plug in new verbs.
  4. Say it out loud. Conjugations you can only recognize on paper won't show up in the speaking section. Read every table aloud at least once.
  5. Revisit old stages weekly. Once you've moved to Stage 3 or 4, spend 10 minutes a week reviewing Stage 1 — foundations decay fastest under new material.
  6. Weight your time toward Stage 4. It's the hardest to automate and the most directly graded in the writing and speaking tasks — don't split study time evenly across all four stages.
  7. Watch the underlined letters. Once you notice that je/tu forms usually end in a silent -s, il/elle forms in a silent -t, nous forms in a silent -s, vous forms in a silent -z, and ils/elles forms in a fully silent -ent, most present-tense pronunciation stops being memorization and starts being pattern recognition.
1

Foundations

lock these in first

Every later stage assumes these are automatic — conjugation, agreement, and basic sentence shape.

  1. Present tense — regular -er / -ir / -re verbstap for tables▸

    Three verb families, three fixed endings. Learn the pattern once and it applies to hundreds of verbs.

    parler (-er), finir (-ir), vendre (-re)
    Personparlerfinirvendre
    jeparlefinisvends
    tuparlesfinisvends
    il / elleparlefinitvend
    nousparlonsfinissonsvendons
    vousparlezfinissezvendez
    ils / ellesparlentfinissentvendent
    Tip: drill the six endings on their own first — e/es/e/ons/ez/ent for -er — then plug in five new verbs a day rather than memorizing verbs one at a time.
    Je parle français. Nous finissons le travail à cinq heures. Ils vendent leur voiture.
  2. Present tense — key irregularstap for tables▸

    These seven verbs show up in roughly half of everyday sentences — memorize them before anything else on this list.

    être, avoir, aller, faire, pouvoir, vouloir, devoir
    Personêtreavoirallerfairepouvoirvouloirdevoir
    jesuisaivaisfaispeuxveuxdois
    tuesasvasfaispeuxveuxdois
    il / elleestavafaitpeutveutdoit
    noussommesavonsallonsfaisonspouvonsvoulonsdevons
    vousêtesavezallezfaitespouvezvoulezdevez
    ils / ellessontontvontfontpeuventveulentdoivent
    Tip: notice peux / veux / dois all end in -x or -s in je/tu — group irregulars by their rhyming forms instead of treating each as a separate puzzle.
    Je suis fatigué. Nous avons faim. Ils peuvent venir demain.
  3. Adjective agreement & placementtap for tables▸

    Adjectives agree in gender and number with the noun, and most sit after it — except a short list that goes before.

    Agreement patterns
    Masc. sing.Fem. sing.Masc. pluralFem. plural
    petitpetitepetitspetites
    heureuxheureuseheureuxheureuses
    blancblancheblancsblanches
    Tip: remember BAGS — Beauty, Age, Goodness, Size (beau, jeune/vieux, bon/mauvais, grand/petit) go before the noun. Everything else goes after.
    Une petite maison blanche. Un bon film. Une femme heureuse.
  4. Negation forms (ne...pas, ne...jamais, ne...plus, ne...rien)tap for tables▸

    Negation wraps around the conjugated verb: ne before it, the second part right after.

    Common negation pairs
    FormMeaningExample
    ne...pasnotJe ne sais pas.
    ne...jamaisneverIl ne mange jamais.
    ne...plusno longerNous n'habitons plus ici.
    ne...riennothingElle ne dit rien.
    ne...personnenobodyJe ne vois personne.
    Tip: with passé composé, most forms wrap around the auxiliary (je n'ai pas mangé) — but personne is the exception and goes after the past participle (je n'ai vu personne).
    Je ne parle pas espagnol. Il n'a rien compris.
  5. Question formation — est-ce que and simple inversiontap for tables▸

    French has three ways to ask the same question — you only need to produce one of them reliably.

    Three ways to ask "Do you speak French?"
    StyleExampleRegister
    IntonationTu parles français ?informal, spoken
    Est-ce queEst-ce que tu parles français ?neutral, safe everywhere
    InversionParles-tu français ?formal, written
    Tip: default to est-ce que on the exam — it needs no word-order changes and is never wrong, so it removes one source of mistakes under time pressure.
    Est-ce que vous avez un moment ? Qu'est-ce que tu penses de ça ?
2

Everyday structures

extend the present

Still present-tense-adjacent, but this is where sentences stop being simple statements and start doing things — planning, judging, comparing, avoiding repetition.

  1. Futur proche (aller + infinitive)tap for tables▸

    The easiest future tense in French: present-tense aller plus any infinitive, no exceptions.

    aller + infinitive
    Personaller+ infinitive
    jevaisparler
    tuvasparler
    il / ellevaparler
    nousallonsparler
    vousallezparler
    ils / ellesvontparler
    Tip: you already know aller from Stage 1 — this "tense" costs you nothing new to learn except sticking an infinitive on the end. Use it by default when speaking about the future.
    Je vais étudier ce soir. Nous allons visiter Montréal.
  2. Devoir / pouvoir / vouloir + infinitivetap for tables▸

    Your three modal verbs for obligation, ability, and desire — each followed directly by an infinitive.

    Meaning & use
    VerbMeaningExample
    devoirmust / have toJe dois partir.
    pouvoircan / mayTu peux entrer.
    vouloirwant toElle veut apprendre.
    Tip: in Stage 4 you'll learn je devrais / je pourrais / je voudrais (conditionnel) — these are the softened, more natural-sounding versions of the same three verbs, so mastering the present forms now makes that stage nearly free later.
    Nous devons finir avant midi. Vous pouvez m'aider ?
  3. Direct & indirect object pronouns (me, te, le, la, lui, nous, vous, leur)tap for tables▸

    Pronouns replace a noun already mentioned and sit right before the conjugated verb.

    Direct vs. indirect object pronouns
    PersonDirect (no preposition)Indirect (to ...)
    me / teme, teme, te
    il / ellele, lalui
    nous / vousnous, vousnous, vous
    ils / elleslesleur
    Tip: the fast test — if the English verb takes "to" (talk to someone, write to someone), use the indirect form (lui, leur). If it doesn't (see someone, watch someone), use the direct form (le/la, les).
    Je le vois demain. Je lui parle souvent.
  4. Comparatives & superlatives (plus/moins/aussi... que, le/la plus)tap for tables▸

    Comparing things is a near-guaranteed vocabulary in the opinion tasks — three structures cover almost everything.

    Comparative & superlative structures
    StructureMeaningExample
    plus ... quemore ... thanplus rapide que
    moins ... queless ... thanmoins cher que
    aussi ... queas ... asaussi grand que
    le/la/les plusthe mostle plus important
    bon → meilleurgood → better (irregular)meilleur que
    Tip: bon → meilleur is the one everyone forgets under pressure and defaults to "plus bon" (wrong) — drill it on its own until it's automatic.
    Ce quartier est plus calme que le centre-ville. C'est la meilleure option.
3

Talking about the past

core of B1 narrative

This is the heart of the writing and speaking narrative tasks — recounting an experience is close to guaranteed on both exams.

  1. Passé composé with avoirtap for tables▸

    Most French verbs form the past with avoir + past participle. Regular participles follow the verb's family; a handful of common verbs are irregular.

    Regular participle endings
    FamilyEndingExample
    -er verbs-éparler → parlé
    -ir verbs-ifinir → fini
    -re verbs-uvendre → vendu
    Common irregular participles
    InfinitiveParticipleInfinitiveParticiple
    avoireuprendrepris
    êtreétémettremis
    fairefaitvoirvu
    direditpouvoirpu
    Tip: learn the 8 irregular participles above as a single block before worrying about regular ones — they're the ones that come up in almost every story you'll tell on the exam.
    J'ai mangé au restaurant. Elle a pris le bus. Nous avons eu de la chance.
  2. Passé composé with être + past-participle agreementtap for tables▸

    A fixed list of ~16 movement/state verbs (plus all reflexive verbs) use être instead of avoir — and their participle agrees with the subject.

    Dr & Mrs Vandertramp verbs
    VerbMeaningVerbMeaning
    DevenirbecomeNaîtrebe born
    Revenircome backDescendrego down
    Montergo upEntrerenter
    ResterstayRentrergo home
    Sortirgo outTomberfall
    VenircomeRetournerreturn
    AllergoArriverarrive
    ——Mourir / Partirdie / leave
    Tip: the mnemonic is literally the name "Dr & Mrs Vandertramp" — each letter is the first letter of one verb. Learn the acronym, not the raw list.
    Elle est allée au marché. Ils sont partis tôt. Nous sommes rentrés tard.
  3. Imparfait — formation and usestap for tables▸

    Take the nous-form of the present tense, drop -ons, and add the imparfait endings. It works for every verb except one.

    parler (regular) vs. être (the one exception)
    PersonEndingparlerêtre
    je-aisparlaisétais
    tu-aisparlaisétais
    il / elle-aitparlaitétait
    nous-ionsparlionsétions
    vous-iezparliezétiez
    ils / elles-aientparlaientétaient
    Tip: être (stem ét-) is the only irregular stem in the entire language for this tense — every other verb, however irregular in the present, follows the nous-form trick.
    Quand j'étais petit, nous habitions à Moncton. Il faisait beau ce jour-là.
  4. Passé composé vs. imparfait — choosing the right onetap for tables▸

    The single biggest source of past-tense mistakes at this level is picking the wrong one. Think camera snapshot vs. video background.

    When to use which
    Use passé composé forUse imparfait for
    a completed, specific actionan ongoing background action
    an event that moves the story forwarda description (people, places, weather)
    something that happened oncea repeated habit ("used to")
    an interruptionwhat was already happening
    Tip: passé composé = the snapshot (what happened); imparfait = the video playing in the background (what was going on). Most narratives use both in the same sentence.
    Je regardais la télé quand le téléphone a sonné.
  5. Sequencing connectors — d'abord, ensuite, puis, enfintap for tables▸

    These four words turn a list of events into a structured story — exactly what the narrative writing task is graded on.

    Narrative sequencing
    ConnectorMeaningPosition
    d'abordfirstopens the story
    ensuite / puisthen / nextmiddle events
    enfinfinallycloses the story
    Tip: use one of these per paragraph in your narrative writing task — it guarantees a visible, gradable structure even if a sentence or two has small errors.
    D'abord, je suis arrivé en retard. Ensuite, j'ai cherché une place. Enfin, la réunion a commencé.
  6. Relative pronouns qui / que / oùtap for tables▸

    Relative pronouns join two sentences into one, which is what separates B1 writing from a string of short A2 sentences.

    qui vs. que vs. où
    PronounReplacesExample
    quithe subject of the clausele livre qui est sur la table
    quethe object of the clausele livre que je lis
    oùa place or timela ville où j'habite
    Tip: the fast test — if a verb comes right after, use qui; if a subject + verb comes after, use que.
    C'est une amie qui travaille à l'hôpital. Voici le film que j'ai vu hier.
4

Opinions, hypotheticals & argument

what B1 tasks actually score

The last stretch: everything the opinion-writing and persuasive-speaking tasks are graded on. Hardest to make automatic, so give it the most practice time.

  1. Futur simpletap for tables▸

    Endings are the present tense of avoir attached to the infinitive — if you know that, this tense is nearly free. A handful of verbs change their stem.

    parler (regular)
    jetuil / ellenousvousils / elles
    parleraiparlerasparleraparleronsparlerezparleront
    Common irregular stems
    VerbStemVerbStem
    êtreser-pouvoirpourr-
    avoiraur-vouloirvoudr-
    allerir-devoirdevr-
    fairefer-venirviendr-
    Tip: the endings (-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont) never change — only the stem does. Learn the 8 irregular stems above as one block; every other verb just uses its infinitive.
    L'année prochaine, je serai étudiant. Nous ferons un voyage en France.
  2. Conditionnel présent — polite requests (je voudrais...)tap for tables▸

    Conditionnel = the same stem as futur simple + the imparfait endings. If you've learned both of those, this one is essentially free.

    vouloir (stem voudr-)
    jetuil / ellenousvousils / elles
    voudraisvoudraisvoudraitvoudrionsvoudriezvoudraient
    Tip: je voudrais, je pourrais, and je devrais instantly sound more natural and polite than the blunt present-tense versions — use them as your default register in speaking tasks.
    Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît. Pourriez-vous m'aider ?
  3. Conditionnel présent — hypotheticals with sitap for tables▸

    For NCLC 5 you only need one si pattern — it's the one examiners are listening for in "what would you do if..." opinion questions.

    Si-clause patterns
    Si-clauseMain clauseMeaning
    si + présentprésent / futura real, likely condition
    si + imparfaitconditionnel présenta hypothetical (this is your B1 target)
    Tip: memorize the shape si + imparfait, ... + conditionnel as one fixed template and reuse it — it's the single highest-value sentence pattern for the opinion tasks.
    Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais davantage. Si je pouvais choisir, j'habiterais à Québec.
  4. Cause & consequence connectors — parce que, donc, car, par conséquenttap for tables▸

    Justifying an opinion is graded directly — these connectors are how you show cause and effect instead of just stating two facts side by side.

    Cause & consequence
    ConnectorMeaningNote
    parce quebecauseanswers "pourquoi ?", can open a sentence
    carbecausemore formal/written, cannot open a sentence
    doncso / thereforeeveryday register
    par conséquentconsequentlyformal, written register
    Tip: use parce que by default when speaking; save car and par conséquent for writing tasks, where they signal a higher register.
    Je préfère le télétravail, car il permet plus de flexibilité.
  5. Contrast connectors — mais, cependant, par contretap for tables▸

    A two-sided opinion needs a contrast word to pivot between the two sides — three options at increasing levels of formality.

    Contrast connectors
    ConnectorMeaningRegister
    maisbutneutral, safest under pressure
    par contreon the other handcommon in speech
    cependanthoweverformal, strong for writing
    Tip: lean on mais when speaking under pressure, and sprinkle cependant or par contre into writing tasks — variety here is one of the easiest ways to raise your writing score.
    Le loyer est élevé, cependant le quartier est très pratique.
  6. Balancing an argument — d'une part... d'autre part, en outretap for tables▸

    This is your ready-made template for the opinion-writing task — it structures a two-sided argument end to end.

    Argument template
    ConnectorRole in the paragraph
    d'une partintroduces the first point
    d'autre partintroduces the contrasting second point
    en outre / de plusadds a bonus supporting point
    Tip: memorize the whole skeleton as one unit: intro → d'une part (point 1) → d'autre part (point 2) → en outre (bonus) → conclusion. Reuse it for every opinion prompt.
    D'une part, le télétravail fait économiser du temps. D'autre part, il peut isoler les employés.

Vocabulary themes

Both exams draw prompts from a narrow band of everyday situations — work through these word banks one at a time before chasing rare vocabulary. Tick a theme once you can use its terms without pausing to translate.

How to build it

  • Learn vocabulary in sentence patterns, not as isolated word lists
  • Read authentic short texts in these themes: notices, ads, articles, emails
  • Retire literal translations from English — learn the French idiom directly
  • Keep a running list of connectors and reuse them until they're automatic
