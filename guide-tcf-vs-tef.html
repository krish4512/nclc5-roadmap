TCF Canada vs TEF Canada

Both tests lead to the same NCLC levels. Here is how they differ and how to choose.

Updated October 2, 2026

Canada accepts two French tests for immigration: TCF Canada (run by France Éducation international) and TEF Canada (run by the Paris Île-de-France Chamber of Commerce and Industry). Both test the same four skills and both convert to the same NCLC levels. The difference is in the format.

Side by side

TCF CanadaTEF Canada
Total timeAbout 2 h 47About 2 h 55
Listening39 questions, 35 min40 questions, 40 min. Each recording plays once.
Reading39 questions, 60 min40 questions, 60 min
Writing3 tasks, 60 min2 tasks, 60 min
Speaking3 tasks, about 12 min2 tasks, about 15 min
ScoringListening and reading out of 699; writing and speaking out of 20Each skill on its own scale (300, 360, 450, 450)

The speaking tasks

TCF Canada

  • Task 1: a short interview about you (about 2 min).
  • Task 2: you ask the examiner questions in an everyday situation (2 min to prepare, 3 min 30 to talk). See the Task 2 guide.
  • Task 3: you give and defend your opinion on a question (4 min 30, no preparation). See the Task 3 guide.

TEF Canada

  • Section A: you call about an ad and ask questions to get information.
  • Section B: you try to convince a friend to do something, using a document you are given.

Which one should you take?

  • Pick the TCF if you like short, predictable tasks. Speaking task 3 can be prepared with a memorised template, and the writing tasks are short.
  • Pick the TEF if you are a strong listener and comfortable persuading someone. The TEF listening plays each recording once, so it rewards quick understanding.
  • Pick the one you can book soonest. In practice, test dates near you often decide. The NCLC level is what counts, not the test.

This site prepares you mainly for the TCF Canada, but the French you learn works for both. Check prices and dates with an approved test centre near you.
